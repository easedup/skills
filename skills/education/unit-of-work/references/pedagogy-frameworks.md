# Pedagogy frameworks reference

Disclosed reference for any skill that builds or reviews a lesson, unit, or
differentiation plan. A teacher may name one of these frameworks as their
school's or their own preferred approach; when they do, apply it as
described below rather than as a label added after the plan is drafted.
Frameworks are not mutually exclusive — Explicit Teaching plus a UDL lens is
a common, sensible pairing, not a contradiction.

If no framework is named, produce a framework-neutral plan using ordinary
evidence-based structure (gradual release, explicit success criteria,
formative checks). Never apply a framework silently — offer it, and only
restructure once the teacher confirms.

Frameworks here carry different kinds of evidentiary weight. Say so plainly
rather than implying every framework rests on the same kind of proof:
**Explicit Teaching** has the strongest controlled-research base (Rosenshine,
AERO). **Visible Learning** and **HITS** draw on large meta-analytic and
department-endorsed evidence bases, with normal caveats about effect-size
heterogeneity. **UbD** and **IBL** are widely-adopted professional consensus
rather than experimentally validated packages. **UDL**'s own research base is
positive but methodologically weak — small samples, one-group designs, and
high heterogeneity in its meta-analyses — so treat "UDL improves engagement
and access" as directional, not as a precise, proven effect.

## Understanding by Design (UbD)

**What it is:** Backward design — fix the desired results and the
assessment evidence before planning any learning activity (Wiggins &
McTighe, 1998). Three stages: (1) desired results, (2) acceptable evidence,
(3) learning plan.

**Evidence weight:** Professional-consensus practice, widely adopted; not an
experimentally tested intervention.

**At lesson level:** Frame lesson goals as understanding goals, not just
topic coverage. Determine the exit task (Stage 2 evidence) before designing
the teaching sequence.

**At unit level:** Lead with Enduring Understandings and Essential
Questions. Position assessment design before the week-by-week sequence.
Make the transfer goal explicit in the unit overview.

**For differentiation:** Because results and evidence are fixed first,
differentiation applies to the path (activities, scaffolds, pacing), never
to the desired understanding itself — every student works toward the same
enduring understanding by a different route.

## Visible Learning

**What it is:** John Hattie's synthesis of meta-analyses ranking
instructional influences by effect size, with feedback, teacher clarity,
and success-criteria co-construction among the highest-yield practices.

**Evidence weight:** Large meta-analytic base; individual effect sizes carry
real heterogeneity and have drawn methodological critique — treat rankings
as directional, not as a precise predictor for any one classroom.

**At lesson level:** Make "so that" appear in every lesson goal (why this
learning matters, not just what it is). Make success-criteria co-construction
an explicit moment in the Introduction phase, not just a statement read aloud.

**At unit level:** Articulate the learning progression clearly — where
students start, what growth looks like, where they end. Surface success
criteria at the unit level, not only lesson by lesson.

**For differentiation:** Frame support and extension as movement along the
same visible progression (surface → deep → transfer), not as separate tracks.

## HITS (High Impact Teaching Strategies)

**What it is:** A set of ten department-endorsed teaching strategies
(explicit teaching, worked examples, feedback, metacognitive strategies,
collaborative learning, differentiated teaching, questioning, and others)
each with a supporting evidence base, used as a menu rather than a fixed
sequence.

**Evidence weight:** Each strategy carries its own separately-evidenced
research base (drawn largely from Hattie and other syntheses); HITS is the
packaging, not a new intervention in itself.

**At lesson level:** Name which HITS strategies are deployed in each phase
of the lesson. Include a planned higher-order question and a genuine
comprehension check — don't just label an existing activity with a HITS name.

**At unit level:** Note which high-impact strategies are deliberately
threaded through the sequence, and flag where metacognitive strategies and
feedback loops are built in across multiple lessons, not just one.

**For differentiation:** HITS names differentiated teaching as one of its
ten strategies — treat this framework's differentiation guidance as a
prompt to open the fuller menu a dedicated differentiation skill produces,
not as a substitute for it.

## Explicit Teaching

**What it is:** A structured I Do → We Do → You Do sequence — teacher
modelling, guided practice, then independent practice — drawing on
Rosenshine's Principles of Instruction and reinforced by CESE's What Works
Best findings.

**Evidence weight:** The strongest empirical base of the frameworks here —
a synthesis of controlled research and master-teacher studies, not a single
tested package, but consistently replicated.

**At lesson level:** Structure the Body phase strictly as I Do → We Do → You
Do, with a modelling script or worked example before students move to
practice. This is also the *default* Body-phase shape even when no
framework is named — it is the floor other frameworks build on top of, not
one option among many.

**At unit level:** Show a clear arc from modelled → guided → independent
across the weeks, with teacher modelling deliberately fading from earlier
weeks to later ones — not just within each individual lesson.

**For differentiation:** Explicit teaching typically comes first while
students build foundational knowledge; UDL-style flexibility and choice
open up once they have that base. Sequence the two rather than treating
them as competing philosophies.

## UDL (Universal Design for Learning)

**What it is:** CAST's framework built on three principles — multiple means
of Representation (input), Action & Expression (output), and Engagement
(task/motivation) — designed in from the start rather than retrofitted for
individual students.

**Evidence weight:** Positive but methodologically weak — small-sample
meta-analyses (e.g. N=18, N=13) with considerable heterogeneity and mostly
one-group designs. Useful as a design lens; don't present it to teachers as
a precisely proven intervention.

**At lesson level:** Add a brief UDL lens note — one option per principle
(representation, action/expression, engagement) built into the plan, not
bolted on as an afterthought.

**At unit level:** Differentiation Considerations should address all three
UDL principles across the whole unit, not just support-and-extension at the
lesson level.

**For differentiation:** The input / task / output structure a
differentiation menu already uses *is* UDL's three principles in practice
(input ≈ Representation, task ≈ Engagement, output ≈ Action & Expression) —
naming UDL here is surfacing the lens already at work, not adding a new one.

## IBL (Inquiry-Based Learning)

**What it is:** Structuring learning around a central question or
provocation that students investigate, with understanding constructed
through the investigation rather than delivered directly.

**Evidence weight:** Widely-adopted professional consensus; where
controlled evidence exists (Hattie's synthesis), it favours the
teacher-guided form specifically — minimally guided/fully open inquiry
shows real risk for students without strong prior knowledge of the topic
(Kirschner, Sweller & Clark; Alfieri et al.).

**At lesson level:** Open with a provocation or essential question. Frame
the body of the lesson as investigation rather than direct instruction.

**At unit level:** Sequence follows inquiry phases (tuning in, finding out,
sorting out, going further, drawing conclusions — or an equivalent cycle).
Essential Questions drive the unit and should be positioned prominently,
not buried after the content list.

**For differentiation:** Guided inquiry — not open inquiry — is the
stronger-evidenced default for most classes; recommend it as the starting
point and let the teacher choose otherwise where it genuinely fits.
